## E. What this means for the design

| What the sources say | Where the programme meets it |
|---|---|
| Staff training on AI risks and requirements [IRS-1, ABA-512, COSO] | Training at each level; Level 1 covers confidentiality, tool limits, secure data handling |
| Documented steps to show adherence [IRS-1] | Evidence register: one row per attempt |
| Client data only in secure, approved tools [IRS-1, FL-1] | Level 1 content; AI policy approved-tools list; gateway controls later |
| Vet third-party AI tools [IRS-1, ABA-512, FL-2] | AI policy; AI gateway project |
| Human review of AI output; the person stays accountable [IRS-1, CPAI, ABA-512] | Every level; assessed in practicals |
| Role-appropriate training [COSO, ABA-512] | Four levels; possible developer track |
| Named policy owner and oversight [COSO, NIST-600, ISO-42001] | Programme owner; AI policy approver `[TBC]` |
| Acceptable use policy [COSO, NIST-600, CPAI] | Policy outline (section F); Level 4 signed statement |
| Agentic risks and controls [OWASP-ASI, SANS-1.4] | Level 4 content; gateway controls before agentic rights are switched on |
| Incident reporting and review [IRS-1, NIST-600, SANS-1.4, FL-2] | Policy outline item 10; Level 4 content |

**Notable points**
- **No source requires a knowledge test.** Training plus records is the common expectation. The test and the champion review are the firm's own choice. They strengthen the "prove why" aim.
- **Human review and personal accountability** is the one point every source agrees on.
- **Agentic content is thin everywhere.** OWASP and SANS are the best sources for Level 4.
- **Policy before levels.** ISO 42001, NIST, COSO and the IRS reading all expect a documented policy with an owner and a review date.
- **Peer programmes teach but do not gate.** The Bridgewater badges have no test or approver. The programme adds both for Levels 3–4.
- **Logging for security is separate from qualification.** SANS recommends logging AI interactions for security. The programme does not use usage data to qualify anyone.

## F. Draft AI policy outline (from the sources; for the firm to confirm or adapt)

1. Purpose, scope (people, tools, devices), owner, approver, review date, link to the levels.
2. Approved tools and how a new tool is requested; vendor vetting.
3. Data classes: what may go into which tool (public, internal, confidential, client, prohibited), with redaction examples.
4. Human review and accountability: the person who uses AI is responsible for the output; verify facts, citations and calculations; second review for client-facing work.
5. Client confidentiality, engagement-letter language and consent, § 7216 and contract terms `[TBC with compliance]`.
6. Acceptable and prohibited uses, with examples.
7. Training and levels (AI Licence Programme); signed acknowledgement.
8. Agentic AI: Level 4 only; approved tools and data only; human approval of irreversible actions; enabled only when gateway controls are live.
9. Documentation: what is recorded in the client file `[TBC]`.
10. Incidents: report immediately; after-action review `[TBC]`.
11. Exceptions, enforcement, policy review cycle.
