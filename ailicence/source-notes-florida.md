## 2. Fla. Admin. Code R. 61H1-23.001 Confidential Client Information (READ, Cornell LII copy)
- Link: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61H1-23-001 (also on the Florida Department of State rules site).
- Text: "A certified public accountant shall not disclose any confidential information obtained in the course of a professional engagement except with the consent of the client." Exceptions: confidential peer-review of the practice; compliance with a lawful court or Board order. Authority: §§ 473.304, 473.315, 473.316, F.S. Amended 2011 and effective 9/11/2018.
- **Relevance:** putting client information into an AI tool that the client has not consented to, or that discloses it to others, is a disclosure risk. Supports "approved tools only", data classes, and a consent/engagement-letter question for compliance. The rule does not mention AI.
- Check whether the current text has changed since 2018 before quoting in a final document.

## 3. Florida Information Protection Act, § 501.171, F.S. (READ, 2025 statute on flsenate.gov)
- Link: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/501.171 (2025 edition; check for 2026 amendments).
- **Applies to JMCO as a "covered entity":** any "commercial entity that acquires, maintains, stores, or uses personal information". "Personal information" = name plus data such as a Social Security number, driver licence or passport number, financial account or other listed elements (full definition in the statute).
- **Duty:** "Each covered entity, governmental entity, or third-party agent shall take reasonable measures to protect and secure data in electronic form containing personal information."
- **Breach notice:** to the Department of Legal Affairs for breaches affecting 500 or more individuals in Florida, and to each affected individual, in both cases no later than 30 days after determining the breach (15 more days with good cause). Third-party agents must tell the covered entity within 10 days.
- **Disposal:** all reasonable measures to dispose of customer records so they are unreadable.
- **Penalties:** a violation is an unfair or deceptive trade practice (enforced by the Department); late notice carries civil penalties of $1,000 a day for the first 30 days, then $50,000 per 30-day period, capped at $500,000 (see § 501.171(9)(b)).
- **Relevance:** if client personal information leaks through an AI tool, this is the breach statute, and AI vendors are "third-party agents". Supports vendor vetting, data-handling rules, an incident process, and limiting agentic access. **Not AI-specific.** "Reasonable measures" is not defined in detail.

## 5. Florida Bar Opinion 24-1 (READ via Internet Archive copy; lawyers only, analogy)
- https://www.floridabar.org/etopinions/opinion-24-1/ (403 to automated fetch; read via web.archive.org). Dated January 19, 2024; advisory ethics opinions are not binding. For **lawyers**, not CPAs.
- Key points: lawyers may use generative AI but must protect client confidentiality, "develop policies for the reasonable oversight of generative AI use", verify work (lawyers remain responsible), keep fees reasonable (no double-billing), and research the tool's "policies on data retention, data sharing, and self-learning".
- **Use:** a Florida-specific parallel to ABA 512. Mention as comparison only.
