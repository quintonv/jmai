## Request for Approval

The Operations Board is asked to approve the following:

1. Migration of firm and CCH documents to Microsoft SharePoint as the firm's primary, governed document management platform.
2. Selection of Intapp as the implementation partner to deliver migration services, the collaboration layer, and system integrations.

## Executive Summary

The firm has already adopted Microsoft SharePoint and has migrated its back-office functions onto the platform. This initiative extends that foundation to our client-facing service lines. It marks the first time our client-facing technical teams will migrate to SharePoint, and the first time we will broadly enable collaboration on client files in SharePoint through Intapp.

Today, critical client-facing knowledge is spread across CCH Document, CCH Engagement, Email, to name a few. This makes documents hard to find, increases compliance risk, and limits our ability to use AI tools effectively.

Migrating Document to Microsoft SharePoint creates a single, secure, and governed home for our documents. (SharePoint is the Microsoft 365 platform we already own for storing and managing files.) Partnering with Intapp adds a collaboration layer that works natively inside Microsoft 365, which reduces migration complexity, speeds up user adoption, and avoids costly rebuilding of our environment.

Intapp Collaboration is Intapp's document management module. It provides the structured, governed, well-organized data foundation that our future knowledge and AI capabilities will rely on. For that reason, this investment should be evaluated primarily on its Day-1 value as a replacement for our current document system (CCH Axcess Document), with knowledge and AI capabilities treated as future opportunities to be approved later under their own business cases as they mature.

This approach allows the firm to:

- Enable AI and Microsoft Copilot on a clean, properly permissioned data foundation.
- Improve security, document retention, and employee controls.
- Reduce time spent searching for and managing documents.
- Lower total migration and change-management costs compared with a custom-built solution.
- Accelerate time to value through faster adoption and reduced disruption.

This proposal recommends approving the Document to SharePoint migration and selecting Intapp as the implementation partner, with a coordinated firm-wide go-live on November 1, 2026.

## Why This Decision Matters Now

### Infrastructure Readiness

The firm's back-office functions are already running on SharePoint, and the firm is now completing the broader SharePoint data foundation, establishing a unified data governance model, and deploying Microsoft Purview and Microsoft Intune to strengthen security and compliance. This initiative extends the proven back-office platform to client-facing files for the first time. Purview gives the firm visibility into where sensitive data lives, how it is used, and who can access it. Intune ensures that all devices accessing firm data (laptops, phones, and tablets) stay secure and compliant with firm policy.

SharePoint is also the foundation required to support Microsoft Copilot, current and future AI integrations. The firm cannot safely realize the expected AI productivity gains until content is consolidated into a clean, well-structured, and properly permissioned environment.

**How the pieces fit together:**

- **Intapp** — business governance, client/matter metadata, and security governance.
- **SharePoint** — document repository and platform.
- **Purview sensitivity labels** — classification and protection enforcement.
- **Intune** — device compliance and access control.

### Governance and Operational Efficiency

Centralizing documents in SharePoint directly addresses urgent governance priorities: document retention, user offboarding, access management, and consistent permissions. The technical work involved (Intapp and CCH integrations, permission mapping, integration testing, data validation, and end-user training) is significant and requires deliberate, phased planning to avoid rushed, high-risk cutovers.

### Cost of Delay

Postponing this decision will result in continued use of CCH Document, which is $200,000 a year with a proposed 8% increase for next year.

Its approach keeps files in SharePoint and adds a collaboration layer that directs users to the correct content and automatically updates links when teams are decommissioned. The table below summarizes how each service line uses documents today. This shaped the future-state design.

| | **A&A** | **ACS** | **Tax** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary Use** | Final audit file / system of record | Ongoing client recordkeeping | Tax preparation + final compliance record |
| **What Goes into Document** | Final workpapers; engagement binders; financial statements; signed reports & audit deliverables | Monthly & quarterly reports; recurring client deliverables; period-based documentation; supporting client records | 1040 & 1041 tax return prep work; filed tax returns; e-file acceptance/rejection reports; signed forms (e.g., 8879s); supporting tax documents |

| **Key Metadata Tags** | Entity (client); storage folder (year); class / subclass (service area) | Entity (client); storage folder (year); class / subclass (service area); document type (month/period) | Entity (client); tax year; class (Tax); subclass (e.g., 8879, acceptance, attachments) |
| **Engagement Binder Usage** | Core structure for audit files; organizes workpapers, testing, and conclusions; supports review, sign-off, and final audit file assembly | Limited / not primary; may be used in select engagements but not central to workflow | Used for business and nonprofit return prep; used for complex 1040 clients (e.g., messy bookkeeping / trial balance work); supports organization of workpapers and trial balance-related documentation |

| **Process / Automation Notes** | Primarily manual save after review and sign-off | Manual + standardized saving aligned to recurring processes | Relies on proprietary automation via J drive network folders to route and file documents into CCH Document; files may originate from users or directly from tax software; documents are linked to Workstream projects for workflow tracking; automated transfer of files between CCH Document and Engagement to support tax prep workflows |
| **What Stays Outside (e.g., OneDrive)** | Drafts, planning, collaboration | Working files, reports in progress | Limited, primarily ancillary work or collaboration files |

### Current Challenges

- **Inconsistent tagging standards:** A&A relies on engagement/binder structure; ACS relies heavily on period-based metadata (month/quarter/year); Tax relies on tax-specific classifications (year, form type, status).
- **Limited cross-service searchability:** Documents are difficult to locate outside the originating service line; similar document types are categorized differently across teams.
- **Reduced AI and automation effectiveness:** Fragmented metadata limits the ability to apply firm-wide search, reporting, and AI tools.
- **Training and adoption challenges:** Users must learn different filing logic depending on the service line.
- **Increased risk of inconsistency:** Variability in how documents are tagged, saved, and retrieved.

## Crawl, Walk, Run Approach

### 1. Future State (Day 1 — Go-Live by November 1)

At go-live, James Moore will have a firm-wide, governed document management environment in SharePoint, enabled by Intapp Collaboration. This is fundamentally a document-system replacement and standardization initiative, not a full AI transformation on day one.

**Key capabilities at go-live:**

- A single system of record for firm and client documents in SharePoint.
- J Drive scripts rerouted to SharePoint-backed storage. (J Drive refers to the firm's existing network drive that automatically files documents.)
- A standardized metadata model across all service lines.
- Controlled permissions, sensitivity labels, and retention policies.
- Integrations to help with document flow.
- Engagement binders remain in CCH Engagement, so service line workflows are not disrupted.

**AI at Day 1:**

- Microsoft Copilot becomes immediately more effective thanks to structured, permissioned data.
- Users can search, summarize, and retrieve documents using natural language.
- AI value at this stage is context-driven, not automation-driven.

At go-live, the firm achieves a clean, secure, and AI-ready content foundation.

### 2. Year 1 Outcomes ("Walk" — Realized Value)

Within the first 6–12 months after go-live, the firm moves from foundational deployment to adoption at scale. This is where measurable return on investment is realized.

**Key outcomes:**

- Firm-wide adoption of standardized document management practices.
- Less time spent searching for documents across service lines.
- Greater cross-service visibility through consistent metadata and search.
- Elimination of duplicate storage and fragmented repositories.
- Improved collaboration through Teams-based workspaces, internal and client-facing.
- Automated workspace creation tied to new client and engagement setup.

**AI value in Year 1:**

- Copilot-enabled document search, summarization, and cross-engagement insights.
- Higher productivity through faster retrieval and contextual responses.
- Early use cases in advisory services (financial analysis, reporting support).
- A foundation for more advanced automation.

### 3. Future "Run" State (Long-Term Vision, 18–24+ Months)

Once the firm's data is fully structured, governed, and consistently used, James Moore can unlock advanced AI and knowledge capabilities. This stage is not required for the initial implementation, but is enabled by it.

**Future capabilities:**

- A firm-wide knowledge layer built from historical engagements.
- AI-driven benchmarking using anonymized client data (e.g., cost, margin, and performance trends).
- Automated document classification, summarization, and insight extraction.
- Standardized deliverables informed by prior engagement intelligence.
- AI-assisted engagement preparation and advisory insights.
- Automated engagement monitoring and closeout workflows.

At this stage, the firm evolves from managing documents, to leveraging knowledge, to driving insight and automation.

## Implementation Plan

### Phase 1: Foundation & Design (July)

**Objective:** Establish the core architecture, standards, and integration design for a successful firm-wide deployment.

**Key activities:**

- Map current-state workflows (A&A, ACS, Tax) to the future-state model.
- Identify service-line champions to own training for their teams: Stacey S. (ACS), Julie L. (Audit), Vanessa A. (Tax), and Kristy M. (back office and service-line backup).
- Define and build LCVista training programs.
- Define retention, sensitivity, and compliance policies; success criteria; and the governance model.
- Finalize the contract, scope, and implementation plan.
- Design the SharePoint architecture (sites, libraries, permissions) and firm-wide metadata standards.
- Design integrations (CCH, Workstream, Outlook, and J Drive rerouting).

**Outputs:** approved architecture and metadata model; governance and security framework; integration design specifications; and a communication and change-management plan.

#### Communication & Change Management

**Channels:** biweekly INFORM email communications, plus a dedicated SharePoint Resource Hub with project updates, training materials, FAQs, timelines, quick-reference guides, and support resources.

**Timeline** (two targeted communications per month, June–October 2026):

- **June — Project awareness and kickoff:** introduce the initiative, objectives, timeline, and expected benefits; establish awareness of upcoming changes and support resources.
- **July — Introduction and early engagement:** provide overviews of Intapp capabilities; encourage users to explore the SharePoint Resource Hub.
- **August — Training and core functionality:** deliver foundational training and demonstrations covering document access, search, filing, and collaboration.
- **September — Readiness and best practices:** reinforce workflows, governance standards, and naming conventions; communicate migration expectations and key dates; gather feedback.
- **October — Readiness through the 10/15 IRS deadline, launch:** final readiness communications, go-live reminders, day-one support information, and escalation paths.
- **Post-launch — stabilization:** ongoing updates, FAQs, office hours, and enhanced support focused on rapid issue resolution and adoption reinforcement after the November 1 go-live.

### Phase 2: Build, Migration & Validation (June–August)

**Objective:** Configure the environment, migrate data, and validate readiness for go-live.

- Configure the SharePoint and Intapp Collaboration environment.
- Build standardized templates (workspaces, document structures, permissions).
- Execute J Drive rerouting to SharePoint-backed storage.
- Develop and test integrations (CCH, Workstream, Outlook).
- Perform scoped, validated, and reconciled data migration.
- Conduct data-quality and permission validation.
- Run User Acceptance Testing (UAT) with super users. (UAT is structured testing by real users before launch.)

**Outputs:** a configured and tested environment; migrated and validated data; approved UAT sign-off; and go-live readiness confirmation.

### Phase 3: Deployment & Training (August–September)

**Objective:** Prepare the firm for transition and ensure adoption readiness.

- Execute role-based training (partners, managers, staff, back office).
- Deliver hands-on training sessions and clinics.
- Communicate new workflows and expectations firm-wide.
- Finalize the cutover plan and support model.

**Outputs:** a trained user base; an adoption support structure; and a completed go-live checklist.

### Phase 4: Go-Live (November 1)

**Objective:** Complete readiness activities through October 15 and execute the firm-wide cutover on November 1, scheduled after the firm's largest October 15 IRS deadlines.

- Execute the system cutover (CCH Document to SharePoint).
- Activate SharePoint as the primary system of record.
- Ensure all integrations are live and functioning.
- Deliver real-time support throughout the transition, in partnership with the operations champions.

**Day 1 outcomes:** a single, governed document platform; J Drive fully rerouted to SharePoint to prevent workflow disruption; Copilot-enabled access to structured firm data; and no disruption to engagement-binder workflows.

### Phase 5: Post-Go-Live Stabilization & Optimization

**Objective:** Drive adoption, optimize workflows, and realize ROI.

- Monitor adoption metrics and user behavior.
- Address support tickets and usability gaps.
- Refine metadata, templates, and workflows.
- Expand collaboration capabilities (Teams workspaces, external sharing).
- Align usage with AI initiatives (Copilot, ChatGPT Connectors).

**Year 1 outcomes:** firm-wide adoption of standardized document management; measurable productivity gains; reduced duplication and improved governance; and increased effectiveness of AI tools through structured data.

## Risk Assessment and Considerations

| Risk | Description | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Adoption & Behavior Risk** | Users continue relying on legacy behaviors (email, local storage, inconsistent filing), resulting in low adoption. | Duplicate systems persist (CCH, J Drive, SharePoint); reduced ROI; limited AI effectiveness; more helpdesk tickets. | Embedded departmental champions; role-based training tied to real workflows; hands-on clinics and post-go-live support; manager KPIs aligned to adoption; active monitoring of usage and search behavior. |
| **2. Data Quality & Metadata Risk** | Inconsistent metadata, poor tagging, or incorrect classification reduces searchability and limits AI effectiveness. | Users cannot reliably find documents; Copilot returns incomplete results; loss of confidence in the system. | Firm-wide standardized metadata model; automated tagging and template-driven creation; validation during migration; ongoing governance and periodic audits. |
| **3. Data Migration & Integration Risk** | Errors in migration, permission mapping, or integrations (CCH, Workstream, Outlook) result in missing, inaccessible, or misclassified documents. | Operational disruption post-go-live; compliance and security exposure; loss of trust in the system. | ITS-led integration sprints (sandbox, mapping, reconciliation); vendor-supported connectors and proven patterns; structured validation and reconciliation; UAT with business stakeholders; defined SLAs and escalation protocols. |
| **4. Workflow Disruption Risk** | Changes to storage and access disrupt established workflows, especially in Tax and ACS where timing is critical. | Reduced productivity during transition; delays in client deliverables; increased support burden. | Deployment timed outside peak seasons where possible; core systems preserved (engagement binders remain in CCH); J Drive rerouting maintains familiar access; targeted validation of high-volume workflows before go-live. |
| **5. Go-Live Execution Risk** | A coordinated firm-wide go-live introduces execution risk if migration, integrations, or user readiness are incomplete. | Firm-wide disruption at go-live; immediate productivity loss; pressure on IT and support teams. | Formal go-live readiness criteria and checklist; controlled validation of representative workloads; a dedicated go-live support model (IT, vendor, and champions); phased preparation with a single coordinated go-live event. |
| **6. Governance & Sustainability Risk** | Lack of ongoing governance leads to inconsistent usage, permission drift, and degradation of the structured environment over time. | Gradual return to fragmented practices; reduced compliance and auditability; declining AI effectiveness. | Defined ownership model (business and IT); ongoing monitoring of usage, permissions, and metadata quality; regular governance reviews; governance built into onboarding and training. |

## Alternative Products Evaluated

Alternatives were reviewed against JMCO's core requirements: Microsoft 365 / SharePoint alignment, governance and metadata support, integration complexity (CCH, Workstream, Outlook, J Drive), migration effort, end-user adoption impact, and total cost to implement and sustain.

### Aiwyn

Strong for workflow, billing, and client-experience use cases, with prior document-management capabilities aligned to Microsoft 365 / SharePoint. However, Aiwyn's document-management offering is being sunset, so it is not positioned as a long-term enterprise DMS and governance platform for this migration. It appears better suited as an operational workflow and client-experience platform than as the strategic governed document layer.

### iManage

A mature, legal- and professional-services-focused document management system with strong governance, security, and document-control capabilities. However, it would introduce a separate content-management platform alongside our SharePoint-centered Microsoft 365 architecture, increasing integration, administration, and change-management complexity compared with a SharePoint-native approach.

### HubSync

Provides workflow automation, client collaboration, and some Microsoft 365 / SharePoint capabilities, but did not offer the same depth of enterprise governance, metadata management, taxonomy standardization, records-management controls, or document-management experience layer targeted for this migration.
